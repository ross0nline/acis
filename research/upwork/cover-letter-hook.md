# Cover Letter Opening Hook

## Strategy

The founder is a board-certified vascular surgeon with 18 years of clinical experience. He didn't build PreClear MD because he read a market report — he built it because he's been on the losing side of prior authorization decisions and knows exactly what a bad outcome looks like for a patient. The hook has to acknowledge that. Generic "I'm excited to build your platform" openers don't land with a surgeon who has seen what's at stake.

Lead with domain resonance, not credentials. Save ACIS and the technical detail for the body.

---

## Draft hook (~100 words — opening of the proposal body)

> Prior authorization is where clinical judgment collides with administrative friction, and physicians lose that fight more often than the system admits. You built PreClear MD because you've lived that — not because someone pitched you a TAM slide. That specificity is why this project is different from most SaaS builds: the product logic, the payer criteria engine, the AI prompts are already grounded in real clinical judgment. What you need is infrastructure that doesn't compromise that judgment with security shortcuts or architecture that can't survive HIPAA scrutiny.
>
> That's the problem I'm built for.

---

## Notes

- "That's the problem I'm built for" is a strong closer for the hook — it pivots directly into the ACIS case study and HIPAA evidence without a clunky transition
- Keep the opener under 120 words — surgeons are time-compressed readers
- Do not mention rate or timeline in the opener; those go in the structured section after
- The phrase "TAM slide" might be too insider; consider "a pitch deck" if unsure of the audience's VC familiarity
- Alternate last line if "built for" feels too strong: "That's the problem I solve."
